# 001-devops-srs: Software requirements specification of the DevOps part

## Problem

The DevOps part of the platform (AWS accounts and network, Kubernetes and delivery, data protection and recovery, security controls, observability, CI/CD and everything managed as code) is described only by the architecture document. That document lives outside the repository, in the Claude project memory, is written in Ukrainian, and mixes requirements with design decisions and operating procedures. Nothing in the repository states, in a form that can be cited and tested, what the DevOps part must provide and how well. Change specifications in `specs/` therefore have no stable requirement IDs to trace their acceptance criteria to, and a reviewer cannot tell a requirement from a design choice.

## Goals and non-goals

- Goal: write the first edition of the software requirements specification of the DevOps part in `docs/requirements/`, structured after ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148, in English, derived from the architecture document, and confirm it as the baseline through the spec gate.
- Goal: every requirement is one table row with a stable never reused ID (`FR-<area>-<n>`, `NFR-<area>-<n>`), one verification method and the technical implementation (tool, resource, setting or number); the text is purely technical, without introductions or explanation of trivial steps.
- Goal: every DevOps requirement of the architecture document appears in the SRS. Precedence on a conflict: the owner decisions below, then section 1 of the source, then the other sections; a contradiction that none of them settles is an open issue and never a requirement. `srs.md` lists every owner decision with the source passage it overrides, and every withdrawn source passage.
- Goal: `traceability.md` maps each in-scope section and subsection to requirement IDs, and each requirement back to a section.
- Goal: after the baseline is confirmed, any change to the SRS is an addendum (`docs/requirements/addenda/README.md`); addenda start only after the baseline has passed the spec gate, and an addendum that changes a requirement cited by an approved specification forces that specification to be re-validated first.
- Goal: Kubernetes resources and Helm charts have one exact place each: `deploy/` is laid out as in section 17 (`charts/`, `envs/`, `platform/`), with the per-chart placement of AC10.
- Non-goal: the business behavior of the payment services (PSP routing, merchant API authentication, webhook signing, routing of payments between cells by hash, card tokenization logic); sections 2 and 6 are in scope only for their platform controls.
- Non-goal: the repository process: section 11; in section 14 the directory tree, directory naming and the glossary extract; in section 18 the directory tree (including the layout of `infra/` and `policy/`), agent models, code comments and refactoring; sections 21.4 and 27.1 to 27.4, 27.7, 27.8; the Claude Code bullet of 21.5. `CLAUDE.md` governs them.
- Non-goal: design rationale and operating procedures (ADRs, runbooks); the SRS links to them as "ADR-0NN (architecture section 13)".
- Non-goal: changing code, infrastructure or policy, and translating the architecture document. The only architecture decision made here is the ADR of T11 that records the owner decisions D6 and D7.

### Decisions of the owner

- D1, version tags: `v*` (SemVer `vX.Y.Z`; breaking change bumps major, `feat` bumps minor, `fix` bumps patch) replaces `rel-<date>.<N>`. Only the tagging workflow creates them, after a successful prod rollout in all cells; creation is allowed and deletion denied by the ruleset; a tag builds nothing and attaches a changelog, SBOM and provenance. The version identifier stays the image digest.
- D2, application databases (Aurora PostgreSQL of every cell and of the locker, DynamoDB with the merchant-to-cell map): a base backup plus point-in-time recovery to any second within the retention; data loss on restore at most 5 minutes; the restore is verified automatically every day; an alert fires when the latest restorable time lags more than 5 minutes for 10 minutes; a policy denies a stateful resource without a backup plan, the written exception list of section 19 being the only way around it. This replaces the weekly restore test and the retention and frequency rows of sections 5 and 19 for these stores.
- D3, other stores and schema changes: MemoryDB, ClickHouse and Velero are backed up once an hour (AWS Backup minimum for MemoryDB) with retention 35 days, replicated across AZs; critical payment data of MemoryDB is duplicated in Aurora within the drain lag SLO; MemoryDB has no point-in-time recovery, so its recovery rests on the multi-AZ log and on rebuilding from Aurora. Before every migration or schema change of any database (Aurora, DynamoDB, ClickHouse, OpenSearch) the migration Job takes an on-demand snapshot labelled with the change and starts the change only when the snapshot is `available`; that snapshot is kept 35 days.
- D4, restore speed: restoring an application database in Frankfurt after a logical error takes at most 30 minutes into prepared capacity (the loss of the working region is recovered by D7, not by a restore); `backup_restore_duration_seconds` is recorded per store with an alert at 80% of the target and a trend against data size. Recent copies stay in hot storage; managed backups (Aurora, DynamoDB, MemoryDB) are compressed by AWS, self-managed ones (ClickHouse, Vault, OpenSearch, Velero) use zstd at a low level so that decompression is fast, compressed before encryption, in parallel streams; restore traffic uses VPC endpoints for S3; the restore role, the KMS key access and the service quotas are prepared and exercised by the daily test; a restore is code (an automation document and Terraform) with no manual step except the approval of its pull request by two people; independent databases are restored in parallel; a restored PostgreSQL database is warmed (`pg_prewarm` of the hot tables) and takes traffic only after readiness.
- D5, storage tiers: backup copies stored as objects (S3-based backups of ClickHouse, Vault, OpenSearch, Velero and exports) move to a colder tier after 7 days and to a still colder tier after 14 days by lifecycle rules in Terraform; the monthly 7-year archive goes to the coldest tier; the restore targets apply to the hot tier, and the restore time of colder tiers is stated per tier. Continuous recovery of Aurora and DynamoDB and managed snapshots that AWS does not tier keep their native tier. AWS limits (for the Infrequent Access classes 30 days in the first tier) are checked at implementation and the days adjusted.
- D6, region model: Frankfurt (eu-central-1) is the only region that writes. High availability inside it comes from three AZs: Aurora quorum and reader promotion, MemoryDB multi-AZ, MSK RF 3, pod anti-affinity and PDB per AZ, and the writer of each cell in a different AZ. us-east-1 is the recovery region: it receives data and backups continuously but runs no instance and no cluster; all compute (EKS, nodes, Aurora instances, MemoryDB, MSK, ClickHouse) is described in Terraform and Terragrunt and created only at the switch. There is no active-active and no standby cluster. The switch is semi-automatic as in ADR-015: detection by independent signals and fencing of Frankfurt are automatic, and one on-call confirmation starts an automated runbook, because a false switch with two writers can charge a payment twice. us-east-2 is the second recovery region: it receives the same data continuously and is used when compute cannot be created in us-east-1 (D7). A new ADR replaces the warm standby in eu-west-1 of ADR-004 and section 3.
- D7, data replication and the switch: Aurora of every cell and of the locker has Aurora Global Database secondary clusters without instances (storage only) in both us-east-1 and us-east-2; an alert fires when its lag exceeds 5 s and the RPO budget is 10 s; managed RPO (`rds.global_db_rpo`) stays off because it pauses commits in Frankfurt when the link is slow. AWS supports this configuration for Aurora PostgreSQL Global Database (a headless secondary, a storage cluster with no DB instance; AWS documents it in "Creating a headless Aurora DB cluster in a secondary Region" in the Aurora guide: for Aurora PostgreSQL it is created through the CLI, the API or Terraform without a DB instance (the console does not support it), compute is not charged and only storage is, and the engine version must be at least 13.4, 12.8 or 11.13, which PostgreSQL 16 satisfies), so the 10 s budget holds without any instance in the recovery regions. DynamoDB uses Global Tables in MREC mode with replicas in us-east-1 and us-east-2, written only from Frankfurt (IAM denies writes in both until the switch). MemoryDB is not replicated across regions: hourly snapshots are copied to us-east-1 and us-east-2, and at the switch it is rebuilt from Aurora and gated by D8; entries not yet drained are lost (at most the drain lag, p99 5 s) and their payments are settled by `sync` with the PSP. Accepted risk (owner decision): a repeated request after the switch, with the same idempotency key, could create a second charge if the key was still in MemoryDB and not yet in Aurora (at most the drain lag, p99 5 s before the failure); the write-behind rule stays, and such a charge is detected by the PSP reconciliation over the 5-minute window with the reference id and refunded. MSK is not replicated (that needs a running cluster): the payment path accepts payments without MSK, events reach S3 through the S3 sink, and Kafka is rebuilt from that archive and ClickHouse is restored from its hourly backup and caught up from it. S3 (backups, audit logs, Terraform state, archives) is replicated to us-east-1 and us-east-2 with Replication Time Control (15 minutes) and Object Lock on every copy; hourly snapshot copies of Aurora, DynamoDB, MemoryDB, ClickHouse, Vault and Velero go to both; container images and charts are replicated by ECR replication to both. Prepared in us-east-1 and us-east-2 in advance, none of it compute: VPC, subnets, TGW attachment, Network Firewall policy, egress Elastic IPs registered with every PSP, VPC endpoints, KMS multi-Region replica keys, IAM roles, Secrets Manager replicas, raised quotas, and the Global Accelerator endpoint group at weight 0. Tools of the switch: the runbook (SSM Automation document), the Terraform state (S3 replica) and the container images are replicated into both recovery regions; the CI runners, Vault (restored from its replicated hourly Raft snapshot) and ArgoCD are created inside the RTO budget; the monitoring that confirms the switch runs from three independent points outside eu-central-1; the VPC CIDRs of the recovery regions are 10.102.0.0/16 for us-east-1 and 10.103.0.0/16 for us-east-2, which do not overlap Frankfurt or each other. RTO budget: detection and confirmation 10, fencing 2, EKS with its base components, runners, Vault and ArgoCD about 25 in parallel with the Aurora instances about 15 and the MemoryDB cluster, then nodes and sync of the cells with the MemoryDB warm-up of D8 (at most 5) 15, traffic switch 2; the critical path is about 54 minutes, so the RTO target is 60 minutes; in plan B the 10 minutes of waiting for capacity are added, so the plan-B RTO target is 75 minutes (the runbook may also start compute in both recovery regions at once and shut down the one that loses the race, which removes the waiting time). Both are measured by the quarterly test of a full switch, to us-east-1 and us-east-2 in turn. RPO: payment data at most 10 s (Aurora) plus undrained entries within the drain lag, with charges confirmed by PSPs recovered by reconciliation; DynamoDB seconds; S3 and the event archive 15 min; snapshot-only stores (MemoryDB, ClickHouse, Vault, Velero) 1 h; Kubernetes state 0 because Git is the source. Plan B, when compute cannot be created in us-east-1: first, inside us-east-1, Karpenter node pools allow several instance families and generations (r7g, r6g, m7g, r7i, r6i; images are built for arm64 and amd64) across all three AZs, and Terraform tries an ordered list of Aurora instance classes (r7g, r6g, r7i, r6i). If that does not help, the same switch runs in us-east-2 from its own replicas, with the same RPO and the plan-B RTO of 75 minutes. The runbook starts compute in us-east-1 and, after 10 minutes without capacity, in us-east-2; the promotion of the Aurora secondary is the point of no return and happens in exactly one region under a lock, and the replicas of the other region then follow the new writer, so that two copies exist again. The quarterly test switches a cell to us-east-1 and us-east-2 in turn.
- D8, cache warm-up (an owner addition that the source does not contain): after a node replacement, an AZ loss or a recovery a cell takes traffic when at least 75% of the most important hot keys (merchant configuration, routing, limits), loaded by priority, are in MemoryDB, measured by `cache_warm_ratio`; the rest of the cache warms under traffic. The readiness probe waits at most 5 minutes; if the ratio is still below 75% the cell takes traffic anyway and raises an alert that does not page the on-call.

## Impact on SLO, PCI and cost

- SLO: none, documentation only; the SRS records the SLOs, and D6 and D7 change the region rows of section 1.
- PCI: none for this documentation; the requirements of D2 to D7 bring recovery regions (encrypted card data of the locker in backups, KMS replica keys, IAM roles, SCP allow list) into the scope of the CDE, so each implementing specification sets `pci-impact: yes` and gets its own PCI review.
- Cost: none now; D6 removes the warm standby, and daily restores of every Aurora cluster, the hourly backups, the 35-day retention and the S3 replication add cost, which each implementing specification estimates.

## Risks

- The architecture document is the only source and sits outside the repository, so the SRS could drift from it. Mitigation: the source path, date and checksum prefix are pinned in `plan.md` and `srs.md`, AC4 and AC5 are checked against it, and `traceability.md` names the section of every requirement.
- Numbers can be mistyped. Mitigation: AC4 lists every value and a reviewer compares them.
- Requirements, design and procedures are easy to mix. Mitigation: the non-goals name what stays out, each requirement is one "shall" statement, and the human reviewer signs off AC7.
- Many owner decisions override the source, so writers could carry over stale text. Mitigation: AC11 lists the overridden and withdrawn passages and T3, T4 and T7 cite that list.
- D7 depends on free AWS capacity at the moment of the switch (no reservation, no standby compute). Mitigation: several instance families and Aurora classes, the continuous replicas in us-east-2 for plan B, and the quarterly test in both regions measures the critical path; the second replica doubles the storage and transfer cost.
- GitHub issues do not exist yet: they are created after the approval, and until then `issue: none`. Mitigation: the spec gate rejects a commit for a task without an issue number.
